## 4. C3 Recalibration on the untouched train split

- Design: test half 3,877 items and calibration pool 3,878. The pool gives **K = 4** disjoint calibration samples (the protocol expected about 5), plus 100 resamples.
- **G4 at harm cost 10:** 0 of 4 disjoint samples and 0% of resamples are over a budget for jev, microsoft and the mean of three. gpt and rounded microsoft are never certifiable.
- **G2 is not reliable:** the mean of three exceeds the harm budget in 3 of 4 disjoint samples and 44% of resamples, and microsoft in 29% of resamples.
- **G1:** microsoft exceeds the ask budget in 2 of 4 disjoint samples and 20% of resamples.
- **Higher harm costs (50, 200):** G4 for the mean of three and for jev mostly falls back to the empty policy (0–2.5% safe autonomy). Microsoft keeps about 3–6%.

## 6. C5 Unseen service (Mailu)

- **Own certification:** Mailu has 69 refusals, but 88 are needed (and 78 asks against 28 needed). It cannot be certified on its own data.
- **Frozen policies:** no Mailu refusal is executed (0 of 69 for each scorer). The frozen policies for jev and the mean of three also execute **no** safe Mailu calls (0% safe autonomy), so their safety on Mailu comes from executing nothing. Microsoft executes 7.8% of safe calls.
- **Gates calibrated on the train pool:** harm is 0 of 69 for every gate except microsoft/G3 (1.4%). Safe autonomy is 0–17.6% (highest: jev/G4 at 17.6%).
- With 69 refusals, even 0 executions give a 95% upper bound of 5.2%, so the data cannot show that the rate is at most 5%.

## 8. What this means for the paper (Protocol Section 6)

These are points for the authors to decide on. No design element has been changed.

1. **Primary result (P4, P5a):** the certified gate G4 holds its guarantees on 7,755 calls that played no part in the design. This holds both for the frozen policies and when recalibrated on four disjoint samples and 100 resamples. The uncalibrated (P1) and uncertified (P5b, P5c) baselines do not hold their guarantees.
2. **G5 claim (P8c, P8d):** on the full pool, G5 certifies only two of four groups and executes about half as many safe calls as G4 (mean of three: 22.9% vs 41.7%), at slightly higher cost (0.83 vs 0.81). The same pattern holds for jev and microsoft. The claim in Section 5.6 and DP4 that G5 is at least as good as G4 is not supported on untouched data. Under Section 6, this must be reported and any change presented as future work. The data do support P8b: G5 lowers harm (0.8% vs 3.9%) where it certifies.
3. **Unseen service (P10b):** inconclusive. The frozen mean-of-three policy executed 0 of 69 Mailu refusals. But it also executed no safe Mailu calls, and 69 refusals are too few for the upper bound to fall below 5%. The paper can say that nothing harmful was executed on Mailu, but not that the policy is certified there.
4. **Minor predictions not confirmed:** P2c (jev under G1 and G2 still executes 6.5–9.2% of safe calls, not ≤ 1%) and P7a (microsoft's G2 atbench harm is 3.3%, not above 5%). Neither affects the main argument.
5. **Methods text:** report K = 4 disjoint samples, the 12 excluded gpt items and that gpt's responses were deterministic in the anchor check.
